Why it's different

Most forecasts take one offshore number and stretch it across a stretch of coast. Surf doesn't work that way.

A model for every spot

Two breaks a mile apart get different numbers, because the model knows the reef, the headland and the canyon between them, from NOAA's depth data.

See why, not just what

Follow each swell's path into the spot on the swell map, and watch what the mix looks like at the break in the lineup view. No black box.

Kept honest by the lineup

Questions

Where does it cover?

The San Francisco Bay Area and Santa Cruz / Monterey Bay today, 31 spots from Bolinas to Carmel. The rest of California is next, region by region.

Do I need a card for the trial?

No. Create an account and you have Pro for 14 days. When it ends you're on the free plan; nothing is ever charged unless you subscribe.

Where does the data come from?

Seafloor depths from NOAA, live swell from CDIP and NOAA buoys, the 7-day swell forecast from NOAA's WaveWatch III, wind from the National Weather Service and tides from NOAA. The wave model on top of them is ours.

How accurate is it?

It's new, and we're open about that. The physics gets the shape of each spot right (which swells it picks up, how much they shrink on the way in); the reports from surfers are how the exact heights get tuned. Every report makes the next forecast better.

Is this safety advice?

No. It's a forecast, and the ocean doesn't read forecasts. Look before you paddle out, and know your limits.
