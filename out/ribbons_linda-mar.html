How to read this

Each ribbon is one swell, following the path it takes as it bends over the real seafloor (NOAA depth data). Its width is the swell's estimated height along the way, so you can see it shrink as it wraps in. The moving dots show the direction of travel.

A dashed line is a swell that's out there but misses this spot. It's drawn from the part of the swell aimed most directly at the spot, and stops at the ring, the closest that part ever gets. The dotted line shows the gap. Darker water is deeper; the thin lines are the 10, 20 and 50 m depth contours.

In the close-up, the thin colored lines are individual wave paths traced on a 10 m depth grid.
